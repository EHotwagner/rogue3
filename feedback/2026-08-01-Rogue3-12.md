## §2 What worked

- The headless render-and-look loop is the most valuable thing in the toolkit for this class of defect. Rendering the shipped frame and looking at it took minutes and showed more than the written defect report contained: not only were doors missing, the room had no walls, no obstacles and no boundary at all.
- **The independent-critic gate paid for itself many times over.** Two fresh-context critics found ten blocking defects across a suite that was 196/196 green, including this milestone's own defect class reintroduced in the audio cue map, and a blank screen on every door crossing that no requirements-derived test could express. Neither critic was told what to conclude; both were given the rubric and the artefacts.
- Rendering a contact sheet over the *whole* inventory rather than only the milestone's subject is what surfaced two of those defects. The elements nobody was thinking about are exactly where the fixed-screen-coordinate placeholders were hiding.
- The fail-closed contracts worked across three independent gates: guarding one reducer broke exactly the six call sites driving it from a state it never depicts; the authored-digest gate named every stale digest with its current value; the cost-driver coverage gate refused green evidence until each new visual element was bound and observed at its declared scale.
- `main-menu`, the one UI route whose hashed source set includes neither `Model.fs` nor `Render.fs`, kept a byte-identical definition digest across both derivation rounds while the other three moved. That is a free check that a digest set moved where the source moved and nowhere else.

## §3 What did not

- The product shipped a game a human could not play, past ten milestones of green evidence. Every reducer was correct. No coverage model asked whether a key a player can press reaches any of them, and the journey vocabulary that exists to report exactly that had its action slot instantiated with `unit`.
- The first implementation round reintroduced the same defect class it was closing: moving the descent into the fixed step made the `floor-descend` cue unreachable from the production route, and the restaged test dispatched the message directly, so it passed green while a player would hear nothing.
- Two restaged acceptance assertions were weakened without anyone noticing, because the staging helper entered a cleared room and wiped the collections the test seeded.
- Making traversal reachable made the M6 camera slide reachable, and it begins one full room away while nothing draws the room being left. Every door a player crossed would have blanked the screen for 0.35 seconds. Nothing in the suite could see it; a rendered frame showed it immediately.

## §4 Findings

#### §4.1 A coverage model over product-defined messages cannot see an action no message reaches

- **Kind:** quality-gap
- **Impact:** The shipped build was unplayable for ten milestones. `TraverseDoor` and `UnlockDoor` were implemented, tested, and dispatched nowhere in `src/`; `KeyChanged` recorded into the input snapshot and dispatched nothing. Every gate was green.
- **Expected:** A product whose acceptance scenarios include door traversal should not go green while no input route reaches the traversal.
- **Observed:** The concrete mechanism is the journey action vocabulary. `JourneyEvent<'key,'pointer,'menu,'effectResult>` reserves a product-owned action slot, and this product instantiated `'menu` with `unit` — one inhabitant, so the vocabulary could express nothing. Verified at the boundary commit: `git show ce95759:src/Rogue3/PerformanceEvidence.fs` types the adapter with `unit` at line 1007, has `| JourneyEvent.MenuAction _ -> JourneyDispatch.Unbound "menu action"` at line 1022, and the only `MenuAction` any script issued was `JourneyEvent.MenuAction ()` at line 1334, which line 1020 binds to `SpawnM6Particles`. So every `Unbound` arm in the file was unreachable by construction. At the same commit `TraverseDoor`/`UnlockDoor` appear in `src/` only as DU cases and reducers, with no dispatch site.
- **Evidence:** file:docs/roguelike-dungeon-crawler-roadmap.md;file:tests/Rogue3.Tests/M11PlayabilityLegibilityTests.fs
- **Version:** FS.GG.Game.Harness 0.12.0; the closed nine-case `JourneyEvent` and its `'menu` parameter are as pinned. Not re-verified against a newer harness, which is unavailable here.
- **Owner:** FS-GG/FS.GG.Game Game.Harness, for guidance that `'menu` is the product's *action* vocabulary rather than a menu-only slot; and the `fs-gg-playtest` skill, for making an `Unbound` arm that no script exercises a reportable condition.
- **Recurrence:** related to feedback/2026-08-01-Rogue3-11.md §4.3, which recorded that a scenario-indexed sweep finds what milestone-scoped suites do not. That is a positive-pattern finding, not a prior occurrence of this defect; the shared shape is that the sweep was indexed over scenarios while each scenario was still discharged over messages. No issue row exists; the tracker returns none.
- **Avoidable cost:** the whole of this milestone, plus a human launching the game and being unable to leave the first room.
- **Disposition:** product fix

#### §4.2 Moving a reducer behind an input route can silently orphan its effect seam

- **Kind:** defect
- **Impact:** The `floor-descend` audio cue became unreachable from the production route inside the very milestone whose purpose was to close that defect class, and the test covering it passed green because it dispatched the message directly.
- **Expected:** An effect cued from a transition should stay audible when the transition moves behind an input route.
- **Observed:** `AudioCues.forTransition` dispatches `directEventCues` on `msg`. M11 folds `DescendFloor` inside the `Tick` handler, so the seam sees `Tick` and never `DescendFloor` on a real descent. An independent critic measured it against the built assembly: a direct `DescendFloor` produced `[PlaySfx (SoundId "floor-descend", 0.8)]`, while the E-key-plus-Tick route produced `[]` on a floor 1 to 2 transition. The `when next.FloorIndex <> previous.FloorIndex` guard added in the same change was dead on the shipped path. The cue is now derived from the transition alongside the door-lock cues, and `M11PlayabilityLegibilityTests.fs` asserts it from `KeyChanged` plus `Tick`, and asserts silence on a refused descent.
- **Evidence:** file:src/Rogue3/AudioCues.fs;file:tests/Rogue3.Tests/M11PlayabilityLegibilityTests.fs
- **Version:** n/a — product defect, introduced and fixed within this cycle.
- **Owner:** Rogue3 product for the defect; the `fs-gg-audio` skill for the general rule, since a message-keyed cue seam is exactly what breaks when a reducer moves behind an input route.
- **Recurrence:** new as an audio finding, but it is §4.1's shape one layer down: the same "prove it by dispatching the message" reflex, in the same cycle that was fixing it.
- **Avoidable cost:** one full implementation round; caught by an independent critic rather than by the suite.
- **Disposition:** product fix
