repository.** See §4.5: they killed all 15 counter mutants, 9 of which the unit suite let through.

0 and leaves `git status --porcelain` showing only files this cycle deliberately edited. On a change
that touches 24 files, being able to trust that signal is worth a great deal.

## §3 What did not

full, including why the guard could not simply be updated and what the rewrite asserts instead.

#### §4.3 A word-boundary regex rename silently rewrites identifiers inside string literals, and the compiler cannot see it

- **Kind:** friction
- **Impact:** any worker performing a bulk identifier rename in this repository. The damage is
  invisible to `dotnet build`, invisible to `dotnet test` when the literal is only a message, and
  *silent* when the literal is compared against a name that also moved.
- **Expected:** a rename tool changes identifiers.
- **Observed:** the 17-field rename was applied with a `(?<![0-9A-Za-z_])NAME(?![0-9A-Za-z_])`
  substitution over `src/**/*.fs`, `tests/**/*.fs` and `scripts/*.fsx`. It correctly avoided every
  suffix case (`EnterM5Room`, `maximumM5Enemies`, `loadM5Room`, `damageM5Obstacle` all preserved) —
  and it also rewrote string literals, which no compiler or test distinguishes from code.
  `git diff ccff119...HEAD` **modifies 21 distinct string literals** that contain a renamed
  identifier. They fall into three classes and only the third matters:
  **prose that should move** — 17 `ScaleSource` strings in `PerformanceEvidence.fs`, correct and
  desirable;
  **a test case name** — 1, in `M5EntitiesBossRoomsTests.fs` (`testCase "the player's blocking rects
  are derived from M5Obstacles with no stored copy"`), harmless;
  **identifier-as-data** — 3 literals in `M5EntitiesBossRoomsTests.fs`
  (`[ "M5Enemies"; "M5Obstacles"; "M5ShopSlots" ]`, a list of field names fed to reflection),
  which is exactly how §4.2 became a self-contradiction rather than a stale-but-honest failure.
  Separating the three classes required a scripted audit of every changed quoted string, because
  the first two are indistinguishable from the third to every tool in the build.
- **Attribution, measured rather than estimated.** Applying the 17 substitutions to the *base* text
  of every changed `.fs`/`.fsx` file and counting the literals that move gives the exact figure:
  **15** literals are rewritten by the substitution alone — 11 `ScaleSource` strings in
  `PerformanceEvidence.fs`, the 3 guard literals and the 1 test case name. The remaining edits are
  the 7 hand-made `"Model.TotalX …"` → `"Model.Instrumentation.TotalX …"` prose changes from the
  counter extraction, whose identifiers never changed name and which no regex could have produced.
  The two sets overlap in exactly one literal — the `collision.shot-wall-queries` `ScaleSource`,
  which mentions both `TotalWallQueries` and `M5Obstacles` — so 15 + 7 − 1 = **21**, which
  reconciles the measured substitution count with the total modified count. **15** is the number
  this finding rests on.
- **Evidence:** command:git diff ccff119...HEAD; command:git show ccff119:src/Rogue3/PerformanceEvidence.fs; file:tests/Rogue3.Tests/M5EntitiesBossRoomsTests.fs; file:src/Rogue3/PerformanceEvidence.fs
- **Version:** n/a
- **Owner:** EHotwagner/rogue3, worker practice for bulk renames
- **Recurrence:** new
- **Avoidable cost:** one scripted audit over the 21 modified literals, plus a second pass applying
  the substitution to base text to attribute them. Small here; unbounded if skipped.
- **Disposition:** accepted as an observation, with the practice recorded and the measurement
  reproducible — after any regex-driven identifier rename, apply the substitution to the base text
  and enumerate the literals it moves, then classify each as prose (should move), message
  (harmless) or identifier-as-data (load-bearing). The third class is the only one that matters and
  it is the one nothing in the build will surface. No handoff is requested; the change surface is
  worker practice, not a component.

#### §4.5 Exact-equality cost drivers, not the test suite, are what proved seven rewritten counters still increment

- **Kind:** positive-pattern
- **Impact:** the whole "zero behaviour change" claim for the counter extraction.
- **Expected:** a refactor is proven safe by its tests.
- **Observed:** it was not, and could not have been. The seven counters have **six** read sites in
  the entire test suite, covering **three** of the seven; `TotalWallQueries`, `TotalHomingQueries`,
  `TotalSecretRevealCandidates` and `TotalDoorSensorQueries` have **no** test assertion at all. Every
  one of the seven was rewritten from a flat `{ model with TotalX = model.TotalX + n }` into a nested
  `{ model with Instrumentation = { model.Instrumentation with TotalX = ... } }`. A dropped or
  mis-targeted increment in four of the seven would have passed the suite green.
  `readiness/performance-evidence.json` is what caught the class. At the re-derivation point every
  workload's *only* failing reason was the stale declaration, with **zero** cost-driver problems;
  at the branch head the same artifact shows all seven workloads `passed: true` with `reasons: []`,
  which is the state a reader reproduces today and is the stronger form of the same fact. Every
  counter's observed value equals its declared `MaximumExpected`. **Six** of the seven are
  `RequiredIn [ "maximum-content" ]`, where `costDriverProblems` demands exact equality rather than
  a ceiling, so a dropped, doubled or mis-targeted increment reds the run:
  `collision.combat-candidates` 2100, `collision.shot-wall-queries` 740,
  `simulation.homing-target-considerations` 2400, `simulation.shot-spawn` 3,
  `simulation.door-sensor-candidates` 8, `simulation.floor-pickup-candidates` 12. The seventh,
  `simulation.secret-reveal-candidates`, is `RequiredIn [ "secret-reveal" ]`, where the check is the
  weaker `observed < MaximumExpected`; declared 1, observed 1, so a *dropped* increment would still

  because the guarantee really is weaker for that one counter.

#### §4.7 The two committed digest families respond to different things, and nothing says so

- **Kind:** documentation
- **Impact:** anyone re-deriving digests in this repository, and specifically anyone who adds a
  comment late in a cycle and is surprised to find the gate red again.
- **Expected:** "the digests" behave as one family.
- **Observed:** they do not, and this cycle demonstrated it by accident. The four **UI-route**
  digests in `EvidenceCommands.fs` hash the *bytes* of their `sourceFiles`, so a pure comment added
  to `Model.fs` moves the three routes that hash it. The seven **workload** digests in
  `PerformanceEvidence.fs` fold in `modelDefinitionFingerprint`, which is `Determinism.encode` of the
  model *value*, plus a `workloadSourceFingerprint` extracted from between the
  `WORKLOAD-SOURCE-BEGIN/END` markers — so a comment moves neither, whether it is in `Model.fs` or in
  `PerformanceEvidence.fs` outside the markers. Adding a late comment block to `Model.fs` therefore
  re-reddened exactly three of eleven digests and left eight untouched. Both behaviours are
  correct; neither is written down where a re-deriver would find it.
- **Evidence:** file:src/Rogue3/EvidenceCommands.fs; file:src/Rogue3/PerformanceEvidence.fs; command:./fake.sh -t PerformanceEvidence
- **Version:** n/a
- **Owner:** EHotwagner/rogue3, `EvidenceCommands.fs` / `PerformanceEvidence.fs` digest documentation
- **Recurrence:** new
- **Avoidable cost:** two extra `PerformanceEvidence` runs and two extra digest copies, both late in
  the cycle after the tree had already been green. The first was incurred by adding a *comment* —
  the cheapest possible edit — and the second by a one-expression self-review fix. Three
  re-derivations of the same three route digests in one cycle is the standing cost of byte-hashed
  evidence over a file that is still under review.
- **Disposition:** doc fix — recorded in `EvidenceCommands.fs` beside the `declared` map as part of
  this change, so the next re-derivation finds it where it is needed rather than in a report.

#### §4.8 `PerformanceEvidence` cannot distinguish a rename from a dropped counter

- **Kind:** defect
- **Impact:** the merge gate's ability to catch the exact failure this cycle was most exposed to.
  Severity is high in principle and zero in practice this time, because the cost drivers were read
  deliberately.
- **Expected:** the target that owns the counter evidence fails when a counter stops counting.
- **Observed:** it does — but only if you get that far. `EvidenceCommands.fs` runs the
  `m7-ui-performance` route check first and returns early on its exit code, and those route digests
  are `SHA256(definition-text ++ File.ReadAllBytes "src/Rogue3/Model.fs")`. Any byte change to
  `Model.fs` therefore reds the target **before a single cost driver is evaluated**. The
  cost-driver exact-equality check — the only thing in the repository that catches a dropped
  counter, since four of the seven have no unit test — becomes reachable only *after* the digest is
  refreshed. A worker who re-derives the digest, sees green and stops has run the load-bearing check
  exactly once, on already-corrected code. An independent code critic reached the check by calling
  `writeExpectedWorkloadEvidence` directly to bypass the short-circuit; that is not a route the
  build offers.
- **Evidence:** file:src/Rogue3/EvidenceCommands.fs; file:src/Rogue3/PerformanceEvidence.fs; command:./fake.sh -t PerformanceEvidence
- **Version:** n/a
- **Owner:** EHotwagner/rogue3, `EvidenceCommands.fs` evidence-command ordering
- **Recurrence:** new
- **Avoidable cost:** none incurred; the exposure is the finding.
- **Disposition:** issue — evaluate cost drivers before, or independently of, the route-digest
  authorship check, so a stale digest and a broken counter are distinguishable failures. Acceptance

## §7 Workarounds still in the tree

**Two `M6`-prefixed type names survive a cycle titled "retire the meaningless M-prefixes".**
`M6Particle` and `M6CameraTransition` in `src/Rogue3/Model.fs` are still prefixed, as are the `Msg`
cases (`EnterM5Room`, `DamageM5Enemy`, `DamageM5Boss`, `DamageM5Obstacle`, `InteractM5Shop`,
`SpawnM6Particles`, `BeginM6RoomTransition`), the functions (`loadM5Room`, `purchaseM5ShopSlot`,
`stepM5Entities`, `stepM6Presentation`, `damageM5Obstacle`) and the performance cost-driver ids
(`state.m5-enemies`, `ai.m5-decisions`, `scene.m6-camera-transition`, `boss.m5-pattern-emissions`,
`projectiles.m5-boss-emitted`, `scene.m6-enemy-*`, `scene.m6-ordered-layers`).

This is deliberate scope discipline, not oversight: #60's acceptance criterion is precisely "no
`M5`/`M6` prefix remains on a **Model field**", and the standing instruction for this repository is
*do not widen*. The two **types** are the closest call, because they are the declared types of two
`Model` fields, so the deferral is recorded as a decision rather than left as a silence — the
boundary and its cost are written at the field declarations in `src/Rogue3/Model.fs`.

Two concrete constraints shaped where the line fell. `M6Particle` cannot simply become `Particle`:
`GameplayVisualInventory`'s `GameplayVisualElement` already has a `Particle` case and both are in
scope together, so the rename trades a meaningless prefix for a real ambiguity. The cost-driver ids
(`state.m5-enemies`, `scene.m6-camera-transition`, …) are in a different category again — they are
pinned identifiers folded into `criticInputDigest` and cited by merged audits, so renaming them
would be a semantic change wearing a cosmetic disguise, and they should stay.

**Removal condition:** a follow-up item covering the two types, the `M5`/`M6` `Msg` cases and the
prefixed function names as one change event — for exactly the per-change-event reason #60 gives for
combining its own two halves, since `Determinism.encode` writes type names and a later rename moves
all 11 digests a second time. **Risk of permanence:** the prefixes now look intentional where they
survive, so a reader may infer a distinction that does not exist — the same defect #60 was filed to
remove, at one remove. The in-code comment is what keeps that risk visible.
