## §2 What worked

**The M11 descent, reused rather than re-invented.** The item's brief said to follow the shape of the trapdoor's `E DESCEND` prompt and M11's door traversal rather than inventing a new one, and that was the right instruction: `playerRoomIntentsIn` already had an interact edge, a `canDescend` guard and a raised-message convention, and `Render` already had `trapdoorReadyScene` drawn from that same guard. The shop branch is nine lines beside the descent, and the affordance is the same idiom with a different verb. Nothing about the fixed-step input model, the `isFirstStep` gate or the message-raising convention had to be rediscovered.

**Running a mutation batch before handing anything to a critic.** Eighteen mutants against a suite that was already green found five survivors, and one of the five was not a missing test at all: it showed that a `List.exists` guard I had written was dead code. That was a code deletion, not a new assertion, and no amount of reading would have surfaced it. Details in §4.2.

**The offscreen `Render.toPng` path.** Six production frames, no window, about ten minutes of harness. It answered the legibility question — is `NEED 13c` in red actually distinguishable from `E BUY` in gold, at the position the plinth is drawn, without the player avatar covering it — which no scene-graph assertion establishes. The host's minimized-window instruction arrived after these were already rendered and required no rework, because the offscreen path was the one the repo's own `scripts/render-m1[13]-frames.fsx` already used.

**`fsgg-coord set-paths` as a way to shrink a collision.** The first `widen` declared `feedback/` and collided with both live siblings on five tokens. Replacing it with `set-paths` naming the three exact feedback files reduced the collision to one shared path — the excuse ledger — which is the genuine, known contention (§4.6) rather than noise. `set-paths` narrowing as well as widening is what made that possible.

## §3 What did not

**The baseline was not green, and the brief said it was.** `origin/main` at `95b1170` fails `./fake.sh build -t Verify` with exit 1. The item brief stated the opposite. Thirty-five minutes and an unplanned widen went into discovering, attributing and absorbing it (§4.1).

**The "still broken" claims for this item were prose, and stayed green.** The brief predicted that tests asserting `#55` as still-broken would go red on the fix, and that updating them was part of the job. They did not go red: all three were comments. The regression net this item needed had to be built from nothing rather than inherited (§4.3).

**Making a dead reducer reachable exposed a live exploit behind it.** Shops restock on re-entry. Harmless while nothing could buy; an unbounded item engine the instant a key press reaches the reducer. Fixing `#55` without this would have shipped a worse product than leaving the shop unreachable (§4.4).

**Five of my own eighteen mutants survived a green 269-test suite** — the reach radius could be shrunk to half a unit, the nearest-slot rule deleted, the multi-step input gate dropped, and the refusal prompt flattened to say `E BUY` in both states — because every one of those was covered only by a test that walked the player to dead centre of a slot and compared two whole scenes for inequality (§4.2).
